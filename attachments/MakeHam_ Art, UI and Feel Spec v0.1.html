MakeHam: Art, UI and Feel Spec v0.1

Companion to Game Spec v0.2. Defines how the game looks, sounds, and feels to play.

1. Style Direction

"Warm stylized realism." Real enough that a Nigerian player recognizes their world (the zinc roofs, block walls, red earth, and generator hum), stylized enough to run on a mid-range phone and look consistent.

  • Reference: the walled integrated-farm render (clean concrete, crisp roof lines, lush greens, soft sun).
  • Mood keywords: sunny, lived-in, colorful, proud, alive, tidy-but-real.
  • Not: gritty, gloomy, cartoonish-chibi, or photoreal noise.
  • Content tone: clean. Family-friendly (target rating about E10+ / Teen). No gore or nudity. Romance stays at dates, gifts, and flirting. Crime, scams, and hardship are shown through story and consequences, not graphic scenes.

3. Camera and World

  • Camera: orthographic, about 35° pitch, 45° yaw. Optional 90° rotation steps.
  • Zoom: 3 levels (close for interactions, mid for play, far for planning and build mode).
  • Grid: 1 m tiles. Walls, roofs, and props snap to the grid.
  • Time of day: dawn, day, golden hour, dusk, night, each with its own lighting and color grade.
  • Weather: clear, rainy season downpours (puddles, darker sky), harmattan haze (dusty, warm-beige tint), and heat shimmer.
  • Ambient life: birds, goats and chickens in yards, passing keke and danfo, laundry on lines, distant generators.
  • Depth cues: soft shadows, ambient occlusion baked into props, subtle depth fog at the far edge.

4. Characters

Proportions: about 5.5 heads tall, slightly stylized (readable face, expressive hands). No chibi.

Customization (clean and inclusive):

  • Skin tones: 12+ options spanning the real range of Nigerian skin tones, lit to look good in all lighting.
  • Hair: low cut, fade, afro, twists, locs, cornrows, braids, bantu knots, wraps, headtie, gele, durag, cap, hijab.
  • Outfits: ankara, adire, kaftan, agbada, buba and iro, native sets, jeans and tee, work uniform, school uniform, church or mosque wear, sportswear, nightlife fits.
  • Body types: 5 options, with fit adjusting automatically to outfits.
  • Extras: glasses, bags, jewelry, watches, shoes, face marks (optional), hats.

Animations (MVP): idle, walk, run, sit, sleep, eat, cook, wash, talk (2 variants), laugh, argue, phone, carry, farm work (feed, harvest), dance (3 styles), wave, celebrate, tired.

Expressions: simple facial morph targets (happy, sad, angry, tired, surprised, flirty) plus emote bubbles above heads.

NPC readability: every NPC has a distinct silhouette (hair, clothing color, or accessory) and a nameplate on interaction.

Technical (3D): about 5,000 to 7,000 tris per character, one 512×512 texture atlas per outfit set, 20 to 25 bone rig.

Low-end fallback (sprites): the same models baked into 8-direction sprite sheets (64×96 px at base) for very low-end Android devices. Characters and outfits stay identical, so only the rendering changes.

5. Environment and Textures

Method: hand-painted textures with light weathering. Use trim sheets and texture atlases to keep draw calls low.

Texture sizes: props 256 to 512, buildings 1024 atlases, characters 512. Use ETC2 or ASTC compression.

Environmental storytelling: painted shop signs, posters, handwritten price boards, phone-charging stalls, "no light" candle corners, football jerseys on lines. Make every street look like someone lives there.

6. UI Design

6.1 Layout (landscape-first, one-hand friendly)

  • Top-left: avatar portrait and needs (hunger, energy, hygiene, mood) as compact rings.
  • Top-right: ₦ balance, date and time, weather icon.
  • Bottom-left: virtual joystick or tap-to-move (player choice).
  • Bottom-right: big context button (Talk, Cook, Sleep, Work). Changes with what you're near.
  • Bottom-center: phone icon opens the in-game phone.
  • Portrait mode: simplified layout planned for later.

6.2 The Phone (main menu as an in-world object)

Apps: Messages (NPCs, family, scams), Bank (balance, receipts, alerts), Contacts, Map, Jobs and Hustles, Notes (goals), Shop, Settings. This fits the world and gives scam events a natural home (fake alerts arrive as phone notifications).

6.3 Interaction and Dialogue

  • Tap a person or object for a radial action menu (max 4 to 6 icons).
  • Dialogue: speech bubble with portrait, then 2 to 4 choice cards. Show the likely effect icon (relationship, money, risk).
  • Negotiation: a patience meter and price slider.
  • Notifications: short toasts (top-center), with big rewards getting a small celebration.

6.4 Visual Language

  • Panels: rounded, warm-cream cards with soft shadow and a thin pattern border (subtle ankara or adire trim).
  • Icons: chunky, rounded, 2 px dark outline, flat color fill, consistent set.
  • Typography: rounded friendly sans (e.g., Nunito or Baloo) for text; a bold display face for headings; tabular numerals for money.
  • Money format: ₦15,000,000 in full; ₦15M in tight spaces. Gold for gains, red for losses.
  • Motion: fast and soft (150 to 250 ms ease-out). Count-up animation on money changes.

6.5 Accessibility and Inclusion

  • Text size slider, high-contrast mode, color-blind-safe status icons (shape plus color).
  • English and Pidgin text options; subtitles for all voice.
  • Large touch targets (min 48 dp).
  • Language-switch ready for Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, and Tiv later.

7. Feel and Juice

  • Camera: gentle follow, smooth zoom, slight shake only on big events.
  • Feedback: coin-pop and POS-beep sounds on payments, a soft chime on goal progress, a tense low tone on scam attempts.
  • Haptics: light tap on actions, double pulse on money, short buzz on danger.
  • Transitions: day-night crossfades, "time passes" fade for sleep and long tasks.
  • Pacing: 1 in-game day is about 10 to 12 real minutes (tunable). Skip and speed-up controls.
  • Animation polish: anticipation and follow-through on key actions (hammering, cooking, farm work), small idle variations to avoid robotic NPCs.

8. Audio

  • Music: original score blending Afrobeats, highlife, and gentle percussion. Layers change by district and time of day (market, nightlife, village, home).
  • Ambience: crowds, roosters, generators, motorbikes, rain on zinc, call to prayer and church bells (respectful, optional volume).
  • Voice: short Nigerian English and Pidgin barks for greetings and reactions, not full voice acting for MVP.
  • UI sounds: soft taps, paper rustle for notes, phone tones.

9. Performance Budgets (low-end Android first)

  • Target: 30 fps on a 2 to 3 GB RAM Android phone; 60 fps on mid-range and up.
  • Draw calls: under 150 per frame; triangles under 150k visible.
  • Install size: under 150 MB; stream later districts as downloads.
  • Offline-first: the game must work with no internet and low data use.
  • Battery: reduce shadow quality and effects on low settings.
  • Graphics presets: Low (baked sprites, minimal effects), Medium, High.

10. MVP Asset Checklist

  • Characters: 2 base bodies, ~25 hair/outfit parts, ~10 NPC variants.
  • Buildings: home (interior and exterior), market stall set, shop, lounge/club, farm lot kit (walls, pond, coop, beds, water tower).
  • Props: about 120 (furniture, food, tools, market goods, vehicles).
  • Animations: about 25 character clips.
  • UI: HUD, phone (8 apps), radial menu, dialogue, 60+ icons.
  • Audio: 3 music loops, 30+ sound effects, ambience beds.

11. Production Pipeline

  1. Concept: moodboards and key art (hand-drawn or AI-assisted concepting), then approval.
  2. Modeling: Blender, using the modular kit approach.
  3. Texturing: Krita or Procreate for hand-painting; a pattern library for textiles.
  4. Rigging and animation: Blender or Mixamo base clips, hand-tuned.
  5. Engine: Godot 4 or Three.js, with a style test scene first.
  6. Style guide: a living document with palettes, UI rules, and do/don't examples.
